# Build authority by connecting technology to organisational capability

## Goal

Bart becomes a recognised executive voice on turning emerging technology into durable product and organisational capability. The work must create relevant executive conversations, not generic reach. LinkedIn earns attention and dialogue. The adaptivespace blog holds the durable body of work. The knowledge base supplies evidence, examples and unfinished ideas.

The core position is:

> Emerging technology creates durable advantage when leaders redesign products, decisions and organisations so that learning compounds without weakening human agency.

This position is already visible in Bart's strongest work. The strategy makes it consistent, distinct and useful to a defined audience.

## Context reviewed

- The [adaptivespace blog](https://blog.adaptivespace.org/) and its [RSS archive](https://blog.adaptivespace.org/rss/).
- Recent essays on compounding organisations, AI transformation, strategic advantage, product systems, trust and organisational design.
- Bart's [LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/bartsuichies/) and recent posts and articles.
- The current channel pattern. Bart publishes durable essays on the blog and often republishes full articles on LinkedIn.
- The current cadence. Fifteen public blog posts appeared between July 2025 and June 2026, with bursts followed by long gaps.
- LinkedIn and Edelman's [2025 research on hidden buyers](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/research-and-insights/b2b-thought-leadership-influence-hidden-buyers).
- LinkedIn's [guidance on effective thought leadership](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/content-marketing/creating-a-thought-leadership-marketing-plan).
- LinkedIn's [creator analytics reference](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1432215).
- `USER.md`, `SOUL.md`, the writing style guide and the repository working rules.

## Constraints and non-goals

The strategy must preserve Bart's judgement and voice. The team can research, challenge, structure, draft and distribute. Bart remains the source of the position and approves every public item.

Quality has priority over volume. A steady rhythm matters, but a weak post damages authority. The system therefore optimises for distinct ideas, credible evidence and executive relevance.

The plan uses the two current channels. It does not add podcasts, video, X, Threads or a second newsletter during the pilot.

The plan does not create the proposed team. It defines the team, its work and the approval gates needed before creation.

The plan does not automate publication without explicit approval. It also does not buy distribution, redesign the blog or replace Bart's knowledge base.

## Approach

### 1. Own one executive problem

The market does not need another broad voice on AI or product leadership. It needs a leader who explains why technology programmes stall inside organisations and how executives can redesign the system around them.

Bart's distinctive territory sits at the intersection of four subjects:

1. Emerging technology: what changes and what remains hype.
2. Product and strategy: where value, ownership and advantage accumulate.
3. Organisational design: how decisions, interfaces and learning loops work.
4. Leadership and people: how trust, judgement and agency survive change.

Each substantial piece must connect at least two subjects. This rule prevents a split between technical commentary and generic leadership advice.

### 2. Focus on one buying group

The primary audience is European CEOs, CTOs, CPOs and COOs in established or scaling organisations. They own a material technology shift and its operating consequences. Their companies have enough structure for coordination to be difficult and enough ambition for change to matter.

The secondary audience is board members, investors, strategy leaders and transformation leaders. They shape executive appointments, mandates and budgets.

Senior product, engineering and people leaders form the practitioner audience. They test the ideas, supply examples and carry them into executive discussions.

The initial audience hypotheses are:

- Executives can buy AI capability faster than they can redesign decisions, incentives and accountability.
- Product and technology leaders need language that connects technical change to commercial and organisational consequences.
- Boards need tests that separate adoption activity from durable advantage.
- Experienced operators distrust generic transformation advice. They respond to specific mechanisms, cases and trade-offs.

The team must test these hypotheses through interviews, comment analysis, search behaviour and inbound conversations. The strategy treats audience fit as evidence, not intuition.

### 3. Build a recognisable argument portfolio

The editorial portfolio has four connected pillars.

| Pillar | Executive question | Bart's angle | Typical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compounding organisations | What remains after the work is complete? | Work must strengthen the next decision cycle. | Operating models, feedback loops, knowledge flows and AI agents |
| Technology as strategy | Where does new technology create advantage? | Efficiency often creates parity. Advantage comes from owned learning and coordination loops. | AI, digital identity, product passports and platform shifts |
| Product systems | How do products create value in changing contexts? | Leaders design conditions for adaptation, appropriation and coherent emergence. | Product architecture, open source, platforms and user behaviour |
| Leadership under change | How do leaders increase speed without weakening people? | Clear interfaces, trust and decision rights beat heroic control. | Team design, incentives, exceptions and executive practice |

Three recurring formats make these arguments easy to recognise:

- **Executive test:** one hard question that exposes a weak transformation assumption.
- **Mechanism:** a named model that explains how an organisation produces an outcome.
- **Field note:** a concrete experience that shows what changed Bart's view.

Every item follows the same editorial logic: tension, inversion, evidence and implication. It must give the reader a decision, test or model they can use.

### 4. Give each channel one job

The blog is the canonical library. It carries the complete argument, evidence and durable models. The initial rhythm is two essays each month. Each essay contains one defensible thesis and one executive implication.

LinkedIn is the discovery and conversation channel. The initial rhythm is three posts each week:

1. One original claim or executive test.
2. One idea adapted from a blog essay or knowledge-base insight.
3. One evidence-led response to a current market development or another expert's work.

The team adapts ideas to LinkedIn instead of copying each full essay. Each blog essay can support three to five distinct LinkedIn posts over several weeks. Each post must stand alone and add a new angle.

Comments are part of the work. The distribution lead identifies ten relevant conversations each week. Bart joins only where he can add evidence, disagreement or a useful frame.

### 5. Turn the knowledge base into an editorial engine

The knowledge base remains the source of truth. The team adds an editorial view rather than creating a second archive.

Each usable insight needs seven fields: source, date, claim, evidence, audience problem, related pillar and publication state. The research curator reviews new entries weekly and proposes connections between them.

The system maintains three queues:

- **Signals:** relevant changes, examples and questions with no thesis yet.
- **Arguments:** a claim with enough evidence to commission.
- **Pieces:** approved work in drafting, review, publication or measurement.

This structure keeps capture separate from commitment. Interesting material does not automatically become content.

### 6. Use a lean team with clear accountability

Bart is the principal. He supplies judgement, lived experience and final approval. Lena acts as managing editor and portfolio owner. She protects focus, resolves conflicts and keeps the system useful to Bart.

The proposed specialist team contains four agents.

| Role | Responsibility | Weekly output | Decision boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audience Intelligence Analyst | Map the audience, monitor market language and test audience hypotheses. | One signal brief and an updated audience evidence log. | Recommends audience changes. Lena approves tests. Bart approves repositioning. |
| Research Curator | Mine the knowledge base, validate sources and build evidence packs. | Two evidence packs and a ranked insight queue. | Rejects weak evidence. Does not invent a viewpoint. |
| Executive Editor | Shape the portfolio, interview Bart and draft in his voice. | A six-week slate, briefs and drafts for approved ideas. | Can challenge structure and claims. Bart approves the position and final copy. |
| Distribution and Learning Lead | Adapt approved work, schedule publication and analyse response quality. | Channel adaptations, engagement prompts and a weekly learning note. | Never publishes without item-level approval. Does not optimise for reach alone. |

Lena runs a 30-minute weekly editorial review. The meeting selects ideas, tests evidence, assigns work and records what the market taught the team.

This team is intentionally small. A separate designer, SEO specialist or community manager would add hand-offs before the strategy has earned that complexity.

### 7. Run one editorial loop

The operating loop is:

1. The audience analyst reports a material problem, question or change.
2. The curator connects that signal to Bart's knowledge base and credible external evidence.
3. Lena selects the few ideas that strengthen the position.
4. The editor interviews Bart when the argument needs experience or judgement.
5. The editor drafts one core piece and its channel adaptations.
6. The curator checks claims, links and quotations.
7. Bart approves, changes or rejects the work.
8. The distribution lead publishes only the approved versions.
9. The team records who responded, what they understood and which questions remain.
10. The learning returns to the audience map and knowledge base.

The team uses one content record from signal to measurement. It does not manage separate plans for the blog and LinkedIn.

### 8. Measure authority, not applause

The baseline covers the previous 90 days where data is available. The first pilot also establishes missing measures.

The primary outcome is the number of relevant conversations created. A relevant conversation involves a target executive, board member, investor or senior operator and concerns Bart's core territory.

The scorecard uses four levels:

| Level | Measures | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience fit | Share of comments, profile views and followers from target roles and organisations | Shows whether the work reaches the right market |
| Idea value | Saves, substantive comments, direct shares, citations and newsletter subscriptions | Shows whether the work changes or supports thinking |
| Relationship value | Relevant direct messages, introductions, speaking requests and repeat interactions | Shows whether attention becomes trust |
| Opportunity value | Advisory, executive, board or partnership conversations influenced by content | Shows whether authority creates strategic options |

Follower growth and impressions remain diagnostic measures. They never become the main goal.

The team reviews performance by argument, audience and format every month. It stops weak patterns after enough evidence, even when those patterns produce easy reach.

### 9. Prove the system in 90 days

The first 30 days establish the baseline, audience map, editorial charter and six-week slate. The team also tags the most useful knowledge-base material.

Days 31 to 60 run the first publication cycle. The target is two blog essays and twelve LinkedIn posts. The team also records qualitative responses and ten audience conversations.

Days 61 to 90 run the second cycle with one deliberate change based on evidence. The final review compares the two cycles and recommends what to keep, change or stop.

The pilot succeeds when the system produces a sustainable rhythm, stronger audience evidence and more relevant conversations. It does not need viral reach.

## Work breakdown

These are proposed child issues. Create them only after Bart accepts this plan.

1. **Map and validate the target audience**
   - Owner specialty: Audience Research.
   - Scope: Build the audience map, interview guide, baseline and hypothesis log. Analyse current followers, commenters, subscribers and inbound conversations where access exists.
   - Acceptance criteria: The map defines primary, secondary and practitioner segments. It records at least ten evidence points and ranks five audience problems.
   - Blockers: None.

2. **Build the editorial view of the knowledge base**
   - Owner specialty: Knowledge Management.
   - Scope: Add the seven editorial fields, define the three queues and tag the highest-value existing insights.
   - Acceptance criteria: The view contains at least 30 relevant insights. Each item has a source, claim, audience problem, pillar and publication state.
   - Blockers: None.

3. **Codify the editorial charter and voice controls**
   - Owner specialty: Executive Editing.
   - Scope: Turn this position, the existing style guide and Bart's strongest work into a practical commissioning and review standard.
   - Acceptance criteria: The charter includes the thesis, pillars, formats, evidence standard, approval gate and ten voice examples drawn from published work.
   - Blockers: None.

4. **Create the six-week pilot slate**
   - Owner specialty: Editorial Strategy.
   - Scope: Select themes, arguments and formats. Define the intended audience, evidence and decision value for every item.
   - Acceptance criteria: The slate contains three blog briefs and eighteen LinkedIn briefs. Every brief maps to one audience problem and one primary measure.
   - Blocked by: Map and validate the target audience; Build the editorial view; Codify the editorial charter.

5. **Produce and approve the first content cycle**
   - Owner specialty: Executive Writing.
   - Scope: Interview Bart, draft the first two essays and prepare six LinkedIn adaptations. Run evidence and voice checks.
   - Acceptance criteria: Bart approves or rejects each item. Approved items pass the source, voice and executive-implication checks.
   - Blocked by: Create the six-week pilot slate.

6. **Publish and learn from the first content cycle**
   - Owner specialty: Distribution and Analytics.
   - Scope: Publish only approved items. Record channel results, relevant conversations and audience signals in the shared content records.
   - Acceptance criteria: Publication matches the approved versions. The learning note separates reach, audience fit, idea value and relationship value.
   - Blocked by: Produce and approve the first content cycle.

7. **Review the 90-day pilot and decide the next system version**
   - Owner specialty: Strategy and Operations.
   - Scope: Compare the baseline and both cycles. Recommend which arguments, formats, cadence and roles to keep, change or stop.
   - Acceptance criteria: The review names the evidence behind each recommendation. Bart receives one decision paper with a proposed next-quarter plan.
   - Blocked by: Publish and learn from the first content cycle and the equivalent second-cycle work.

## Risks and mitigations

- **The audience remains too broad.** The pilot tests role and problem hypotheses before expanding the portfolio.
- **The output sounds synthetic.** Bart supplies the judgement. The editor uses interviews and published examples. Bart approves every public item.
- **Volume weakens the ideas.** The two-essay rhythm is a ceiling during the pilot. The team can publish less when evidence is weak.
- **Research becomes a second content factory.** Lena commissions only ideas that strengthen the core position.
- **LinkedIn reach distorts the strategy.** The scorecard gives audience and relationship value more weight than impressions.
- **Automation creates reputational risk.** Item-level approval remains mandatory. The distribution lead publishes exact approved versions.
- **The team creates too many hand-offs.** Four specialist agents use one record and one weekly review. Bart has one approval queue.

## Deferrals

- Create or hire the proposed team. This starts only after Bart accepts the plan.
- Create implementation child issues. The accepted plan must define their final scope first.
- Add paid LinkedIn distribution. Organic evidence must identify the strongest arguments and audiences first.
- Add new channels or a LinkedIn newsletter. The pilot first proves the existing blog and LinkedIn system.
- Automate publication or connect private systems. These actions need separate access and approval.
- Commission design support. Add it only when visual formats prove material to idea value.
